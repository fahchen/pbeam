# ERTS Differences

PBeam follows the ERTS implementation model and observable behavior. This file lists each intentional difference from ERTS and OTP. Add a row in the same PR that adds a difference. Remove the row in the same PR that removes the difference.

| Area | PBeam behavior | ERTS behavior | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scheduling | One scheduler thread. At each schedule-in, a process gets a budget of `CONTEXT_REDS` (4000) reductions. The cost points are the points of the pinned ERTS JIT. A function entry costs 1, and the process switches out after the entry when the budget is then 0 or less. A `return` costs 1, and the process switches out after the return when the budget is then less than 0. A `call_ext` of a light BIF and the `send` instruction cost 1 before the call, and the process switches out before the call when the budget is then 0 or less. The call then runs again in the next turn. A `call_ext` of a heavy BIF, a BIF through its export (`apply`, `apply_last`, or a fun call), and a `bif` or `gc_bif` instruction cost 0. A tail call of a BIF through `call_ext_last` or `call_ext_only` then costs 1 for its `return`, and a tail call through the export costs 0. A local send then uses 4 reductions, and returns its value after a switch when the budget is then 0. A NIF call costs the 1 of the function entry. A BIF that traps to Erlang code switches out the process at the trap, and the entry of the target function costs 1 in the next turn. The signal queue has the ERTS parts (row "Signal queue"): a message that arrives is not fetched, and a receive reads only the inner part. `loop_rec` costs 0 when a message of the inner part is next. At the end of the inner part, it fetches and costs 4, also when no message arrived, or, when the middle part has signals, it handles them with a limit of 100 reductions. Thus a receive that waits costs 4 at the wait and 4 again when a message wakes the process. The process switches out before `loop_rec` when the budget is 0 or less, and `loop_rec` then runs again in the next turn; it also runs again in the next turn when the signal handling used up the budget. `loop_rec_end` costs 1. `process_info/2` of the caller, `recv_marker_reserve`, and `erlang:hibernate/0` fetch with no cost. Each handled signal counts 1 and the extra count of its kind, and the handling costs the count divided by 4, plus 1, reductions. A signal to a process that waits puts it in the run queue, except a monitor signal, which waits until its sender switches out. At schedule-in, a process with signals handles them before Erlang code, with at most 100 reductions when it has Erlang work, else with its budget; it switches out with no Erlang code when it has no Erlang work or no budget left. A process that a signal ends at schedule-in exits in that turn, and in `loop_rec` it exits in its next turn. `lists:member/2` and `erlang:length/1` take at most 16 elements for each reduction of the budget, `lists:reverse/2` and `erlang:'++'/2` take 60, `erlang:'++'/2` with `[]` on the right takes 32, and `lists:keyfind/3` and `lists:keymember/3` take 40000 with no cost. When elements remain, the BIF uses its budget and traps to its export. The process switches out, and the export continues the BIF with no cost in the next turn. The guard BIF `length/1` keeps its trap state and runs the instruction again. A process that switches out goes to the end of the run queue and continues where it stopped. `erlang:yield/0` costs 0 and always switches out the process. `erlang:bump_reductions/1` costs 1 as a light BIF, and then uses the given number of reductions, but not more than 4000, down to a budget of 0. Each switch out adds the reductions of the turn to the reduction total of the process, except the 4 virtual reductions of each local send. `process_info/2` with `reductions` gives this total, and for the caller also the reductions of its current turn. Each item of `process_info/2` costs the caller 1 after the read. `integer_to_list/1` and `integer_to_binary/1` cost 1 for a bignum, and return a runtime error for a bignum when no reductions remain. `maps:merge/2` of two maps that are not both flatmaps costs one reduction for each 8 steps of the ERTS hashmap merge. At the end of the budget, it uses all reductions and traps to an internal export, which continues with no cost in the next turn. PBeam terms share no memory, so PBeam counts the steps of a subtree that both maps share, also for `maps:merge(M, M)`. `erts_internal:map_next/3` takes the pairs and the keys that the budget permits, and costs 4 for each pair and 32 for each key (row "Map iteration"). `ets:insert/2` with a list of two or more objects, or with an improper list, uses 8 units for each reduction: 1 for each object of the check, then 2 for each object of the insert when the units that remain are at least the number of objects, else 1 for each object of a copy and 2 for each object of the insert. A failed copy removes the copies with 1 unit for each object. At the end of the budget, it uses all reductions and traps to an internal export (row "ETS tables"). `persistent_term:get/0` costs 1 for each persistent term, and traps at the end of the budget (row "Persistent terms"). `iolist_size/1` and `iolist_to_iovec/1` walk the iodata with 8 steps for each reduction: one step for each cell and for the end of each list, and in the copy of `iolist_to_iovec/1` one more step for each 32 bytes of a binary of at most 512 bytes. A walk costs one reduction for each 8 steps, rounded up. When the steps end, the BIF uses all reductions and traps to an internal export, which walks the same cell again in the next turn. The items of `process_info/2` that PBeam supports cost 1 each, as on ERTS. `maps:from_list/1` and `maps:keys/1` count the work units of the ERTS YCF code: 40 and 15 units for each reduction. A loop step uses one unit, a step of a loop in one loop two units, and a step of a loop in two loops four units, and the selection sort of a sort part of at most 16 items uses `n * bits(n) * 2` units. When the units reach the budget, the BIF uses all reductions and traps to an internal export, which continues with no cost in the next turn. A `badarg` of `maps:from_list/1` costs nothing. The sort of `maps:from_list/1` takes its pivots from the address 0 and the seed 1, so its cost for more than 32 distinct keys is one of the costs that ERTS can give. `persistent_term:put/2` of a new value costs one reduction for each 32 slots of a table copy, traps at the end of the budget, and switches out the process (row "Persistent terms"). `unicode:characters_to_binary/2` and `unicode:characters_to_list/2` run the ERTS chardata walks with 10 loop steps for each reduction: the `utf8_need` walk costs one reduction for each 50 steps, the conversion one for each 10 characters or bytes, and the list build of `unicode:characters_to_list/2` one for each 10 characters. At the end of the steps, the BIF uses all reductions and traps. The exit of a process bumps 100 reductions, uses 80 for each BIF timer to the process, and uses 50 more when it leaves the process table. Then each link, each monitor or alias that it holds, and each monitor of another process or of a TTY resource on it uses 1. Each walk of these entries and of the timers checks the reductions before each entry, and a walk that ends with no reductions left gives 1. The monitors of other processes and of TTY resources on the process go in the order that the process handled their monitor signals, and a TTY resource monitor signal that the process did not handle runs its TTY monitor when the exit answers the signals. The exit then answers the signals in its queue: each signal counts 1, and a monitor signal 5, with a limit of 4 counts for each reduction left. When the reductions end at a check, the exit switches out and continues in its next turn with a new budget and no new bump. Before it leaves the process table, the process keeps its name. After the timers, the exit deletes the ETS tables that the process owns, in creation order, from the reductions left in the turn: each segment of the ERTS hash table of a table uses 32, and 1 for each object in it, from the top segment down. The number of segments and the segment of each object come from the active buckets of the table and the ERTS key hash. The exit switches out when the reductions are less than 0 after a segment or 0 after a table, and the deletion does not change the count of the later exit phases in the turn. A table loses its name and its reference when its deletion starts. The exit cancels the BIF timers in the post-order of the red-black timer tree of the process. These parts are not implemented yet. A local send of a message of more than 64 heap words costs one reduction for each 64 words that ERTS copies, but PBeam terms keep no sharing and no literal origin, so PBeam charges no copy. The backlog entry "Model the process heap for GC reductions and the message copy cost" of `docs/backlog.md` owns this cost. `maps:from_list/1` that finds a bad element or tail after a trap returns a runtime error. | Timers | PBeam has one timer queue on the run clock. The run clock is the ERTS internal monotonic time: one second at the run start, in nanoseconds, from `std::time::Instant`. When no process can run, PBeam sleeps until the first timer end, or waits for fd 0 until then when fd 0 has a read select (see "Input select").
